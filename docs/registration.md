# Australian registration research

Checked 10 October 2026 using official sources. Australia is the working
jurisdiction; the applicant and licence jurisdiction still need confirmation.
No agency has been contacted and no application has been submitted.

## Distinct milestones

Publishing the open-source application is a software-release milestone. An
application, expression of interest and formal accreditation are different
regulatory milestones and must have separate dates in the comparison.

Australia's voluntary Digital ID accreditation scheme regulates organisations
and their services against privacy, security and other standards. It is not a
simple registration of a facial matching algorithm.
[Digital ID accreditation](https://www.digitalidsystem.gov.au/digital-id-accreditation)

## ACCC Digital ID accreditation

The current application guidance is Version 1.3, May 2026, although the download
retains an older filename. Applicants first email the Digital ID Regulator for
submission instructions. The package includes organisation/service forms,
accreditation forms, supporting evidence and declarations. Identity proofing at
IP2 Plus or higher requires biometric testing reports from experienced external
testers uninvolved in the service. Independent security and fraud assessments
and penetration testing are also part of the evidence requirements. The guidance
describes a completeness check, assessment and written decision; it does not
publish an initial accreditation processing deadline. Its 90-day period concerns
internal review of an existing decision.
[ACCC application guidance, sections 3, 5, 6, 9.6, 10 and 11](https://www.digitalidsystem.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/ACCC%20Digital%20ID%20Guidance%20-%20Applying%20for%20Accreditation%20v1.1.pdf)

Joining the Australian Government Digital ID System is a further approval
process. The current official page says private-sector entities can apply from
December 2026. Accreditation alone is not approval to participate in AGDIS.
[AGDIS participation](https://www.digitalidsystem.gov.au/the-australian-government-digital-id-system-agdis)

## Government identity-record services

A private-sector identity service provider can apply for Document Verification
Service access through a nominated approved gateway. IDMatch states a formal
application decision is sent in 2-3 weeks after submission. Eligibility, gateway
arrangements and the participation agreement are additional steps. The DVS
checks document details against issuer records; this timeline is not a promise
for biometric accreditation.
[IDMatch identity service provider process](https://www.idmatch.gov.au/organisations/identity-service-provider)

The Face Verification Service organisations page currently limits access to
approved governments. Private-sector access is described as a future expansion;
organisations can register interest. An expression of interest is not approval
or access to government photographs.
[IDMatch eligibility](https://www.idmatch.gov.au/organisations)

## Independent algorithm evaluation

NIST FRTE 1:1 is a separate performance evaluation track with a participation
agreement, API and validation process. It can supply an algorithm benchmark;
it is not Australian Digital ID accreditation and does not replace service-level
privacy, security, enrolment or liveness evidence.
[NIST FRTE 1:1](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt11.html)

## Applicant details needed before a submission

- Applicant entity, authorised officer and jurisdiction.
- Intended identity-proofing service and target assurance level.
- Whether the aim is ACCC accreditation, DVS access, independent testing, or more
  than one of these distinct outcomes.
- Actual enrolment/document source, consent and data-handling design, independent
  assessment evidence and operational responsibility.

The comparison should record project start, working release, complete application
accepted, accreditation decision and licence received independently. The
software cannot supply an accreditation completion date in advance.
